Decision memo · navigation & SEO

Does the category hero need to repeat itself?

A UI/UX review flagged that /collections/[category] shows a full hero band — same as the homepage's — right before the product grid. Here's the flow as built, the case for changing it, and where TOHFA landed.

Decided — keeping current flow Reviewed 11 Sep 2026 Site tohfaonline.com

01 · The flow as built

Every category link — the homepage's Shop by Category rail, the header's hidden-categories menu — lands on /collections/[category]. That route renders the exact same <StorefrontPage> the homepage uses, just handed a category prop.

Three things change under that prop: the hero swaps from generic copy to a hand-written heading, tagline and intro for that one category; a breadcrumb appears; and Bestsellers / Testimonials drop out in favour of the filtered grid. The Shop by Category rail itself stays on the page, so a shopper can jump sideways to a different category without a trip back home.

02 · Why it's built this way

The comment above the hero in StorefrontPage.tsx is direct about it: each category needed its own unique H1 and intro, or every collections URL would be the same template wrapped around a different filter — the kind of thin, duplicate content search engines actively rank down. The per-category copy is a real investment: eighteen categories, each hand-checked against what's actually stocked rather than assumed from the name.

03 · The case, both ways

Keep the full hero

  • Not everyone arrives via the rail. Search results, a shared link, a bio link land straight on /collections/lamps — for that visitor there was no prior hero, and this is the only one they see.
  • It's the SEO payload. Delete it and every collections page becomes the same grid with a different filter — the exact duplicate-content problem it was built to avoid.
  • Sets context fast. One representative product photo plus a line of copy orients a shopper before a wall of thumbnails does.
  • Keeps the brand band consistent across every page that renders it, rather than some pages having it and some not.

Skip straight to the grid

  • Real repeat, for rail traffic. A shopper who scrolled past the homepage hero once sees a visually similar band again seconds later.
  • Costs mobile scroll. Hero + breadcrumb + the category rail sit above the grid — real distance between a tap and the first product card, on the majority device.
  • Reads as a reset. Same layout, new words — can feel like the page reloaded rather than filtered.

04 · The call

Owner's decision

Keep the current flow.

The rail-click repeat is real, but it's the minority path. Direct and organic landings — the traffic the hand-written copy was built for — never saw a prior hero, and removing it would trade their experience for the rail-click shopper's, while giving up the SEO uniqueness. As built, the hero is doing more good than harm.

05 · On the shelf, not off the table

Two smaller moves would address the rail-click friction without touching the SEO copy. Neither is built — logged for whenever this comes up again.

A

Slim the category hero. Breadcrumb, heading, one line of intro — no full-height photo band. Keeps the unique H1 for search, cuts the vertical space by more than half.

B

Scroll the rail's own click to the grid. The homepage rail already does a client-side navigation — pointing it at the grid instead of the page top would get a rail-click shopper to products immediately, while a direct landing still gets the full hero.
